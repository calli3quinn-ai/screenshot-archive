A twelve-week digital exhibit

More, More, More

A collection of the messages that tell us what to want, what to need, what to think, and what to do.

Vision Statement

What happens when every suggestion starts to feel like a rule?

Six ideas guide this twelve-week collection.

Select a section to explore the vision behind the collection.

01

The Name

More, More, More

The title reflects the pressure to keep up and become a better version of ourselves, even when that improvement comes with instructions to follow everyone else.

02

The Central Idea

Improvement, according to whom?

My idea began with the unnecessary things celebrities and influencers try to convince us to buy. As I thought more about the project, I realized I was interested in the wider set of instructions attached to this content: what we should want, how we should live, and even what we should think.

Posts about AI fit when they tell us which tools we need to keep up or encourage us to let those tools decide what to say or do. What connects these subjects is the expectation that someone else can tell us what a better version of ourselves should look like.

Content promising to help us improve ourselves often encourages us to rely on someone else’s judgment.

Something we had no use for can become a supposed necessity, and following a trend can start to feel like the only way to keep up. What interests me is where a suggestion starts to feel like a rule for living.

I want to examine the tension between being told to become more capable and being encouraged to question less. Across twelve weeks, I will look for that pattern while also including posts that encourage disagreement, independent choices, or satisfaction with what we already have.

03

The Capture Rule

What belongs in the collection?

Each day, I will capture one public post involving celebrity culture, influencer culture, or AI that tells people what to want, need, think, or do, or challenges those instructions.

To qualify, a post must contain an identifiable direction or expectation in its wording, caption, or visual instructions. Examples include a “must have” product, advice to copy a celebrity’s lifestyle, claims about what everyone should believe, or encouragement to let AI make a decision.

Straight celebrity news, an ordinary photograph, or a technical AI update without that message will not count. A post that only makes a lifestyle look appealing will qualify if I can identify a cue establishing an expectation, such as “habits of successful people” or “everyone needs this.” An attractive image alone will not qualify.

04

The Process

How I will capture it

I will look on TikTok and Instagram for public posts, Reels, and advertisements involving these subjects. I often scroll online early in the morning, then mid-afternoon, and finally after 10:00 p.m.

I will take the first qualifying post I encounter. If needed, I will search terms such as “must haves,” “successful habits,” or “AI productivity,” noting when a capture comes from a search.

I will save each screenshot and record the account, platform, link, and a sentence identifying its message. I will upload captures and fuller annotations twice a week. If I miss a day, I will record the gap and reason, then take an additional capture the next day using its actual date.

05

For the Reader

Notice the directions hiding in the feed

By December, I want a reader to notice how much of an ordinary feed is made up of directions. Some are obvious, like being told to buy something. Others are wrapped in encouragement, entertainment, or promises to make life easier.

Seeing them together should make the repetition more noticeable, along with the ways fame, popularity, and claims about technology are used to make advice seem trustworthy. My annotations will identify the instruction and what it promises, whether that is acceptance, success, convenience, or a better appearance.

I hope readers leave more aware of when advice helps them make a choice and when it pressures them to follow along. I want them to consider how they can be encouraged to constantly improve themselves while also being encouraged to let others decide what improvement should look like.

06

Guardrails

Public, purposeful, and fair

I will use only publicly available content and will not include private messages, restricted account posts, or another person’s private communication.

I will crop out unrelated notifications and identifying details from ordinary users’ comments. I will only include material I am comfortable showing this class.

My annotations will address what a post communicates without personal attacks or unsupported assumptions about its creator’s intentions.
